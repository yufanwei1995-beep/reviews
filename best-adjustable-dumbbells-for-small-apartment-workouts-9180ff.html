What to Look For in Apartment Adjustable Dumbbells

Before I get to the reviews, here are the four things that matter more than anything else when you live in a shoebox.

1. Noise Level (The "Don't Get Evicted" Factor)

This is the big one. Dial-based dumbbells (like the Bowflex) use a twisting mechanism that rarely clanks. Pin-based ones (like the PowerBlock) are silent when you put them down but can rattle slightly if the handles are loose. Plate-loaded ones (like the Nuobell) are quiet too, but you have to be careful not to drop them.

Rule of thumb: If you have hardwood floors or downstairs neighbors, avoid anything that requires you to slam weights down. Buy a thick rubber mat regardless.

2. Footprint and Storage

Some dumbbells come with massive cradles that take up more space than the weights themselves. The Bowflex 552 cradle is bulky (about 2.5 feet long). The PowerBlock Sport 2.4 sits in a compact square base that fits in a corner. The Nuobell 32 has no cradle at all — you just twist to select weight and put it on the floor.

Measure your under-bed clearance or closet shelf height before buying. Some of these are too tall to slide under a standard bed frame.

3. Weight Range and Increments

For most beginners and intermediates, 5 to 50 pounds per hand is plenty. If you're a serious lifter, look for the 80-pound or 90-pound options. Pay attention to the increments — the Bowflex jumps 5 pounds after 25 lbs, which is annoying for progressive overload. The Nuobell goes up in 2.5-pound increments all the way up, which is much better for slow gains.

4. Build Quality and Warranty

You're going to slam these down. You're going to sweat on them. Look for metal handles (not plastic) and a warranty of at least 2 years. Bowflex offers a 2-year warranty, PowerBlock offers 10 years (on the frame), and Nuobell offers 1 year — which is short, in my opinion.

The 5 Best Adjustable Dumbbells for Small Apartments

1. Bowflex SelectTech 552

Best Overall

The Bowflex 552 is the gold standard for a reason. It adjusts from 5 to 52.5 pounds per hand with a simple twist of the dial. The mechanism is smooth and, more importantly, quiet. There’s no clanking metal plates hitting each other when you set it down.

The handle is knurled metal — it feels solid in the hand even when you're doing heavy rows. The cradle is bulky, but it doubles as a storage rack. I kept it under my coffee table and it didn't look terrible.

The downside: The weight jumps from 25 to 30, 35, 40, 45, and 50. That 5-pound jump is brutal for lateral raises or curls where you're already struggling at 25. Also, the cradle is heavy and takes up about 2.5 square feet of floor space.

Who it's for: Most people. If you want one pair of dumbbells that does everything well and won't get you evicted, this is it.

2. PowerBlock Sport 2.4 (Adjustable 24)

Best for Speed

The PowerBlock uses a pin system — you slide a steel selector pin into the weight you want and lift. It's arguably faster than twisting a dial, and the design is incredibly compact. The whole set of two dumbbells (5–50 lbs per hand) sits in a footprint smaller than a shoebox.

These are built like tanks. The frame is welded steel, and the handle is a smooth, non-knurled grip that some people love (no calluses) and others hate (slippery when sweaty).

The downside: The shape is weird. They're square and blocky, so you can't rack them on your thighs for a proper curl. Also, the expansion kits are expensive. If you buy this and want to go heavier later, you're paying $150+ per expansion.

Who it's for: People who want to change weights in 2 seconds flat and have almost zero storage space.

3. Nuobell 32 (NÜOBELL)

Best for Range of Motion

This is the one I personally use at home. The Nuobell looks and feels like a traditional dumbbell — no weird square shape, no bulky cradle. You twist the dials on the ends to select weight, and it goes from 5 to 32 pounds in 2.5-pound increments.

The 2.5-pound increments are a game-changer. You can actually progress your curls without making a 5-pound jump. The handle is knurled steel with a perfect diameter (32mm) that feels great in the hand.

The downside: It only goes up to 32 pounds per hand. That's fine for most upper-body work but useless for heavy squats or deadlifts. Also, the build quality isn't as robust as the Bowflex — the plastic dials feel a bit cheap, and the warranty is only 1 year.

Who it's for: Anyone who values precise weight increments and wants a dumbbell that feels like a "real" dumbbell in the hand.

4. Yes4All Adjustable Dumbbells (5-in-1)

Best Budget Option

This is a simple, no-frills plate-loaded dumbbell. You get a single handle and a set of plates that you screw on and off. It takes about 30 seconds to change weight, which is slow, but it costs a fraction of the dial-based options.

For $100, you get a handle that goes up to 50 pounds (or more, depending on the kit). The grip is rubber-coated, which is decent, and the plates are covered in a thin plastic coating so they don't scratch your floor.

The downside: It's slow. You have to unscrew the locking collar, pull off the plates, put on the new ones, and screw it back. If you're doing supersets, this will kill your momentum. Also, the plates rattle slightly during use.

Who it's for: Beginners on a tight budget who don't mind slow weight changes and don't need more than 50 pounds.

5. ATIVAFIT Adjustable Dumbbell

Best for Space-Saving Cradle

The ATIVAFIT is a budget-friendly knockoff of the Bowflex design. It uses a similar dial system, goes from 5 to 25 pounds per hand, and costs about half the price of the Bowflex.

The cradle is smaller than the Bowflex, which is nice, and the weight increments are more granular (5, 7.5, 10, 12.5, 15, 17.5, 20, 22.5, 25). The handle is smooth metal — not knurled, but it's okay for lighter weights.

The downside: The plastic gears inside the dial mechanism feel flimsy. I've seen multiple Amazon reviews reporting that the weight selector strips out after a few months of heavy use. It's fine for light workouts, but don't drop these.

Who it's for: Someone who wants the Bowflex experience but only does light to moderate training and wants to save $200.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are adjustable dumbbells safe for hardwood floors?

None of these are "safe" if you drop them from chest height. You absolutely need a rubber gym mat (at least 1/2 inch thick) underneath. The Bowflex and Nuobell are the gentlest on floors because they have flat, stable bases. The PowerBlock has hard plastic corners that can scratch if you drag them.

Can I do a full-body workout with just one pair of adjustable dumbbells?

Yes. With 50 pounds per hand, you can do goblet squats, Romanian deadlifts, overhead press, rows, curls, and lunges. You won't be setting powerlifting records, but for general fitness and muscle maintenance, it's more than enough.

How long does it take to change weights on each type?

Dial-based (Bowflex, Nuobell, ATIVAFIT): 3-5 seconds per dumbbell. Pin-based (PowerBlock): 2-3 seconds. Plate-loaded (Yes4All): 30-45 seconds per dumbbell. If you do circuit training, the dial or pin systems are worth the extra money.

What's the best adjustable dumbbell for someone who wants to go heavy?

None of these go above 90 pounds per hand. If you need more than that, look at the PowerBlock Pro 90 or the Ironmaster Quick-Lock system. They're bulkier and less apartment-friendly, but they get the job done.

The Bottom Line

If you can only buy one pair, get the Bowflex SelectTech 552. It's the best combination of quiet operation, build quality, weight range, and ease of use. You'll pay around $350, and it'll last you years. If you're on a tighter budget and don't mind slower weight changes, the Yes4All is a solid $100 starter set. And if you're a home-gym snob who wants precise increments and a traditional dumbbell feel, spend the extra cash on the Nuobell 32 — just accept that you'll outgrow the 32-pound max eventually.
