What to Look For in Apartment Dumbbells

Not all adjustable dumbbells are apartment-friendly. Here's what actually matters when you're working out in a shared building.

Weight Range

Most budget sets go from 5 to 25 pounds. That's fine for curls and lateral raises, but you'll stall on presses and rows within a few months. Look for at least 50 pounds per dumbbell if you're a man, or 40 pounds if you're a woman. The Bowflex 552 tops out at 52.5 pounds, which covers 90% of home lifters.

Change Speed

In a small space, you don't want to spend 90 seconds unscrewing collars between sets. Dial systems (Bowflex, PowerBlock) change in seconds. Screw-lock systems (like the Yes4All) take 30-45 seconds per dumbbell. That's annoying during a circuit but fine for straight sets.

Noise Level

This is the dealbreaker. Plate-loaded dumbbells clang when you set them down. Dial systems are quieter but the weight plates can rattle during reps if the locking mechanism isn't tight. The quietest option is the PowerBlock design — the weights are enclosed in a metal cage, so there's no loose plate rattle at all.

Footprint

Measure your floor space. A 50-pound dumbbell pair takes up about 2 square feet when stored. The Bowflex cradle is bulky but sturdy. The PowerBlock stand is narrow but tall. If you're storing under a bed or in a closet, get the Yes4All — they collapse into two small cases.

Durability

Cheap dumbbells use plastic gears that strip after a year. Look for metal or reinforced nylon locking mechanisms. Read the warranty — Bowflex offers 2 years, PowerBlock offers 10 years on the frame. That tells you who trusts their product.

1. Bowflex SelectTech 552

Price: $349 (often on sale for $299)

Rating: 4.5/5

Best for: The all-around pick for most apartment lifters.

The SelectTech 552 is the gold standard for a reason. You twist a dial to select from 15 weights (5 to 52.5 pounds in 2.5-pound increments). The change takes about 3 seconds per dumbbell. The cradle holds both weights and keeps them off the floor.

Build quality is solid — the plates are metal, the dial mechanism is plastic but has held up in my tests for years. The noise level is moderate. When you set them down on a mat, the plates clack together slightly. It's not silent, but it won't wake the baby.

The biggest downside is the length. At 16.5 inches, they're longer than standard dumbbells at heavier weights. That makes some exercises (like close-grip presses) feel awkward. Also, if you drop them from overhead, the plastic dials can crack. Use a mat and control your reps.

Bottom line: The best all-around option. Durable enough for daily use, fast enough for circuits, and the 2.5-pound increments are perfect for progressive overload.

2. PowerBlock Elite EXP

Price: $399 (with stand, $449)

Rating: 5/5

Best for: Quiet workouts and heavy lifters who need up to 90 pounds.

PowerBlock uses a completely different design — the weights are stacked in a metal cage and you pull a pin to select the weight. No dials, no loose plates. The result is the quietest adjustable dumbbell I've tested. There's zero rattle during reps, and setting them down is a soft thud.

The Elite EXP starts at 5 pounds and can expand from 50 to 90 pounds with add-on kits. That's a huge range for a tiny footprint — the whole thing sits in a stand that's about 18 inches square.

The tradeoff is the shape. The rectangular cage feels different in your hand than a traditional dumbbell. It's 6 inches wide, so exercises like dumbbell flys have a wider range of motion. Most people adapt within a week, but it's not for everyone.

The pin system takes about 5 seconds to change. The magnetic handles are a nice touch — you can swap in a knurled handle for heavy lifts.

Bottom line: If noise is your #1 concern or you need more than 50 pounds, this is the one. The 10-year warranty tells you it's built to last.

3. Yes4All Adjustable Dumbbells (Screw Lock)

Price: $199 (up to 50 pounds per dumbbell)

Rating: 3.5/5

Best for: Budget buyers with storage space under a bed or in a closet.

This is the cheapest way to get a real 50-pound dumbbell. It's a classic screw-lock design — you twist the collar to remove plates and slide on heavier ones. The plates are rubber-coated, which cuts down on noise and protects your floors.

The big advantage is storage. The whole set collapses into two small plastic cases that slide under most beds. That's a lifesaver in a tiny apartment.

The downsides are real. Changing weights takes 45 seconds per dumbbell. That's fine for straight sets but infuriating for supersets. The collars can loosen during reps if you don't tighten them enough — I've had a plate slide off mid-curls. Always double-check the tightness.

Also, the 2.5-pound plates are sold separately, so you're stuck with 5-pound jumps unless you spend extra.

Bottom line: A great value if you're on a budget and don't mind slow weight changes. Just be careful with the collars.

4. NÜOBELL 32

Price: $599

Rating: 4/5

Best for: People who want a premium look and feel without the PowerBlock boxiness.

The NÜOBELL looks like a traditional dumbbell. It's a dial system that goes from 5 to 32 pounds in 1-pound increments. Yes, 1-pound jumps — that's incredible for progressive overload on things like lateral raises where 2.5 pounds is too much.

The build quality is exceptional. The handle is knurled steel, the plates are rubber-coated, and the dial mechanism is smooth. It's the nicest-looking dumbbell on this list — it doesn't scream "home gym."

But the weight cap is the problem. 32 pounds is fine for upper body isolation work but useless for squats, rows, or presses. If you're a beginner, this might be all you need. If you're intermediate, you'll outgrow it in 6 months.

Noise is low — the plates are coated, so there's minimal clanking. The footprint is similar to the Bowflex but slightly shorter in length.

Bottom line: The premium choice for beginners and women who focus on upper body. The 1-pound increments are a game-changer, but the weight range is limiting.

5. Bowflex SelectTech 1090

Price: $649

Rating: 4/5

Best for: Serious lifters who want up to 90 pounds without buying PowerBlock expansion kits.

This is the bigger brother of the 552. It goes from 10 to 90 pounds in 5-pound increments. Same dial mechanism, same cradle design, but much heavier and bulkier.

The weight range is the story here. 90 pounds per dumbbell handles any home workout, including heavy rows and presses. The dial change is just as fast as the 552 — about 3 seconds.

The downsides are significant for apartment dwellers. At 17.5 inches long, these are huge. You can't do close-grip presses comfortably. They're also heavy — the pair weighs 180 pounds total, so you need a sturdy cradle (included) and a floor that can handle it. The noise is the same as the 552: moderate clacking on the plates.

Also, the 5-pound increments feel coarse. Going from 50 to 55 pounds is a big jump for smaller lifts.

Bottom line: Best for heavy lifters with a reinforced floor. Skip this if you're doing mostly upper body isolation work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a mat for adjustable dumbbells?

Yes. Even the quietest dumbbells make noise when set down on hardwood. A 1/4-inch rubber mat (like the Heavy Duty Rubber Mat from Rubber-Cal) absorbs impact and protects your floor. It also prevents the dumbbells from sliding.

Can I do all exercises with adjustable dumbbells?

Almost. You can't do heavy barbell squats, but you can do goblet squats, lunges, and Romanian deadlifts. The main limitation is that adjustable dumbbells are longer than standard ones, so some exercises feel different. You'll adapt.

How do I keep the noise down for my downstairs neighbor?

Three tips: use a mat, don't drop the weights, and set them down with control. The PowerBlock is the quietest design because the weights are enclosed. The Bowflex will clack if you let it.

Are these safe for hardwood floors?

Yes, if you use a mat. The Bowflex cradle sits on four rubber feet. The PowerBlock stand is stable. The Yes4All plates are rubber-coated. Just don't drop them on bare wood.

Bottom Line Verdict

For most people living in a small apartment, the Bowflex SelectTech 552 is the right call. It's fast, durable, has a wide enough weight range, and doesn't break the bank. If noise is your biggest fear or you need more than 52 pounds, get the PowerBlock Elite EXP — it's quieter and more expandable. On a tight budget? The Yes4All screw-lock set works fine as long as you can handle slow changes. Skip the NÜOBELL unless you're a beginner, and skip the 1090 unless you have a reinforced floor and a serious squat game. Measure your space, buy a mat, and start lifting.
