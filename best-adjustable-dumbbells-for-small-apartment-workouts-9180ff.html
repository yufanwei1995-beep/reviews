Who this is for: Apartment dwellers, small home gym owners, and anyone who needs serious strength training without a rack of iron cluttering their living room.

The problem: Traditional dumbbells take up a massive footprint, are loud when dropped, and just look ugly in a small space. You need something compact, quiet, and effective—without sacrificing build quality.

  • Our Top Pick: Bowflex SelectTech 552 — the best balance of price, range, and durability.
  • Budget Pick: Yes4All Adjustable Dumbbells — basic but reliable, perfect for lighter use.
  • Price Range: You'll spend between $150 and $600 depending on features and weight range.

What to Look For in Adjustable Dumbbells

Buying the wrong pair is a $400 mistake. Here's what actually matters for apartment workouts.

Weight Range and Increments

Most adjustable sets go from 5 to 52.5 lbs per dumbbell. That covers most home lifters. But check the increments. Some adjust in 5-lb jumps (like the Bowflex), which can feel like a big leap when you're curling 20 lbs. Others, like the SMRTFT, adjust in 2.5-lb increments—much smoother progression for smaller muscle groups.

If you're a serious lifter, look for a set that goes to 80 lbs or more. But understand that heavier sets are bulkier and harder to store.

Locking Mechanism

This is the safety issue that most reviews skip. The quick-adjust dials on Bowflex and PowerBlock systems are secure when used correctly. But they can loosen over time. The screw-lock style on budget sets requires more effort but never accidentally releases.

I've seen cheap knockoff dials fail mid-lift. That's a dropped weight on your foot, or worse, on your floor. Stick with trusted brands.

Noise and Floor Protection

In an apartment, noise is the #1 complaint. Some adjustable dumbbells clank when you set them down because of loose internal plates. The PowerBlock system is notably quieter because the plates are stacked tightly. Also, consider buying a rubber floor mat—it's a small investment that saves your downstairs neighbors' sanity.

Storage Footprint

Measure your space before you buy. The Bowflex SelectTech 552 comes with a cradle that's about 17 inches wide. The PowerBlock takes up even less space—just two small blocks. Budget sets (like Yes4All) store the plates on a tray, which takes up more room but is still more compact than 10 individual dumbbells.

The 5 Best Adjustable Dumbbells for Apartments

1. Bowflex SelectTech 552

Price: $349-$399 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best For: Most people

The Bowflex SelectTech 552 is the gold standard for a reason. It adjusts from 5 to 52.5 lbs in 5-lb increments using a simple dial system. Turn the dial, and the weight locks into place. It's fast, it's intuitive, and it's been on the market for over a decade because it works.

The build quality is solid. The molded handle is comfortable, and the plates are covered in a protective coating that won't scratch your floors. The cradle is compact—about the size of a large shoebox—so it fits in a closet or under a bed.

Tradeoffs: The 5-lb increments are annoying when you're curling 15 lbs and want to move up. Also, at 52.5 lbs, it's not enough for heavy leg work. And the plastic housing can crack if you drop it from shoulder height. Be careful.

2. PowerBlock Elite EXP

Price: $399-$449 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best For: Quick weight changes

PowerBlock uses a different system. You slide a selector pin into the stack of metal plates you want, and lift. It's faster than turning a dial, and the expansion packs let you go from 50 lbs up to 90 lbs later.

These feel different in your hand. The handle is a fixed metal bar, and the plates are arranged in a square block. It's a bit weird at first, but you get used to it. The weight distribution is actually better than traditional dumbbells because the mass is centered.

Tradeoffs: The square shape makes them terrible for exercises where you rest the dumbbell on your thighs (like goblet squats). And they're pricey. But the expansion capability makes them the smartest long-term investment on this list.

3. NÜOBELL 32

Price: $599-$699 | Rating: 4/5 | Best For: Design lovers

The NÜOBELL looks like a modern sculpture, not gym equipment. It uses a dial adjustment system similar to Bowflex, but the plates are completely encased in a sleek plastic shell. It's the most aesthetically pleasing option for a living room that doubles as a gym.

The weight range is 5 to 32 lbs per dumbbell. That's a limitation. You'll outgrow these quickly if you're doing serious pressing or squats. But for curls, shoulder work, and general conditioning, they're perfect.

Tradeoffs: The price is steep for the weight range. And the locking mechanism can be finicky—you have to make sure the dial clicks fully into place before lifting.

4. SMRTFT Adjustable Dumbbells

Price: $249-$299 | Rating: 4/5 | Best For: Budget-conscious lifters

SMRTFT offers a 2.5-lb increment adjustment, which is rare at this price point. The dial system is smooth, and the plates are covered in a protective rubber coating that's gentle on floors.

The weight range is 5 to 25 lbs per dumbbell. That's fine for upper-body work and light leg exercises, but it's not enough for serious lower-body training. The build quality is good for the price, but the plastic parts feel less premium than Bowflex.

Tradeoffs: The 25-lb max is a hard ceiling. If you think you'll progress beyond that, spend more upfront. Also, the handle is slightly thinner than standard, which can be uncomfortable for people with large hands.

5. Yes4All Adjustable Dumbbells

Price: $150-$200 | Rating: 3.5/5 | Best For: Absolute budget shoppers

Yes4All takes a simpler approach. You get a set of weighted plates, two handles, and two locking collars. You manually screw the plates on and off. It's more work, but it's the most affordable way to get a full dumbbell set in a small space.

The weight range goes up to 100 lbs per dumbbell if you buy the right set, which is more than any of the dial-based options. The plates are standard metal, so they clank when you set them down. Buy a mat.

Tradeoffs: The screw-on collars take time to adjust—you won't be doing drop sets with these. And the plates can loosen during exercises like cleans or snatches. Check the collars frequently.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are adjustable dumbbells safe for apartment floors?

Yes, if you use them correctly. The coated plates on Bowflex and NÜOBELL won't scratch wood or tile. PowerBlock's metal plates can leave marks if you set them down hard. Always use a rubber mat, regardless of which model you choose.

How much weight do I actually need?

For most beginners, 50 lbs per dumbbell is enough for the first 6-12 months. After that, you'll need more for lower body movements. If you're already lifting, go straight to a set that maxes out at 80+ lbs.

Can I drop these dumbbells?

No. The plastic and metal mechanisms in adjustable dumbbells are not designed to withstand drops. Dropping them can break the locking mechanism, which is a safety hazard. If you need to drop weights, use a gym.

Do they take up less space than regular dumbbells?

Absolutely. A full set of traditional dumbbells (5-50 lbs) takes up a rack that's 6 feet wide. Adjustable dumbbells take up less than 2 square feet. That's the whole point.

Bottom Line Verdict

For 90% of apartment dwellers, the Bowflex SelectTech 552 is the right choice. It has the best balance of weight range, build quality, and price. The 5-lb increments are a minor annoyance, but the reliability and track record make it the safest buy. If you're on a tight budget and don't need more than 25 lbs, the SMRTFT is a solid budget pick with finer increments. And if you plan to get serious about lifting, save up for the PowerBlock Elite EXP with an expansion kit—it's the only one that grows with you past 50 lbs. Skip the NÜOBELL unless aesthetics are your top priority, and skip the Yes4All unless you're okay with the manual adjustment hassle.
