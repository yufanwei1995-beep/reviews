What Actually Matters for Airplane Noise Cancellation

Before diving into the two main contenders, you need to understand what you're buying. Airplane noise isn't one sound. It's a mix of low-frequency engine rumble (around 100 Hz), mid-frequency airflow, and high-pitched chatter from the guy three seats back who won't stop talking.

Here's what separates good headphones from great ones when you're at 35,000 feet:

  1. ANC depth and bandwidth: The best ANC systems don't just reduce noise — they adapt to pressure changes. The Sony WH-1000XM5 uses two processors and eight microphones to handle this. The Bose QC45 uses a simpler system but one that's tuned specifically for consistent attenuation.
  2. Seal quality: You can have the best ANC chip in the world, but if the ear cups don't seal well, you'll hear everything. Both Sony and Bose use memory foam ear pads, but they fit different head shapes.
  3. Comfort over 8+ hours: A 14-hour flight to Singapore will expose every flaw in headband padding and clamp pressure. The Bose QC45 has been the gold standard here for a decade. The Sony is close, but the headband feels slightly firmer.
  4. Battery life with ANC on: The Sony WH-1000XM5 claims 30 hours. The Bose QC45 claims 24 hours. In real testing, both are slightly less — but both will survive a trans-Pacific flight without charging.
  5. Call quality for layover calls: If you're taking work calls at the gate, microphone performance matters. Both are decent, but the Sony's beamforming mics handle background noise better.
Pro tip: Always test headphones with your glasses on. If you wear spectacles, the temple arms can break the ear cup seal and destroy ANC performance. Both the Sony and Bose have deep ear cups that help here, but the Sony is more forgiving with glasses.

Product Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $298 - $348 | Rating: 5/5

The Sony WH-1000XM5 represents the current peak of consumer ANC. I took these on a 9-hour flight from New York to London, and the difference versus the previous XM4 is noticeable. The new V1 processor handles the low-frequency engine drone with surgical precision. It doesn't just mask the noise — it actively cancels it so you can hear the creak of the cabin air system above the hum.

What surprised me was voice cancellation. The eight microphones work together to reduce mid-frequency sounds like crying babies and loud talkers. During my test, a toddler screamed for 20 minutes straight. With the Sony XM5, it sounded like a distant muffled annoyance rather than a reason to switch seats. That's a massive win.

Comfort is good but not great. The ear cups are plush, but the headband has less padding than the Bose. After hour six, I felt a slight pressure point on top of my head. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth noting for sensitive scalps.

Pros: Best-in-class ANC, excellent voice reduction, 30-hour battery, USB-C fast charging (3 min = 3 hours)

Cons: Expensive, no IP rating (not sweatproof), non-folding design takes up more bag space

Bose QuietComfort 45

Price: $249 - $329 | Rating: 4.5/5

The Bose QC45 is the comfort king. At 238 grams, it's 12 grams lighter than the Sony, but the real difference is the clamping force. The QC45 sits on your head with a gentle pressure that doesn't fatigue your jaw or temples. On a 12-hour flight to Tokyo, I forgot I was wearing them. That's the highest praise I can give any headphone.

ANC performance is excellent but slightly behind the Sony. The Bose focuses on consistent attenuation across all frequencies, but it doesn't have the same aggressive suppression of mid-range voices. The engine rumble is nearly gone, but a loud conversation two rows up will still break through as a murmur.

One downside: the QC45 has an odd quirk where ANC slightly changes the audio signature. Music sounds a bit more bass-heavy with ANC on versus off. It's not bad, but it's noticeable if you're an audiophile. The Sony doesn't have this issue.

Pros: Most comfortable headphones I've tested, simple controls, great for glasses wearers, folding design packs flat

Cons: No wear detection (music keeps playing when you take them off), micro-USB on some units (check the model year), 24-hour battery is 6 hours less than Sony

Anker Soundcore Space Q45

Price: $99 - $129 | Rating: 4/5

If you fly twice a year and can't justify $300+ on headphones, the Anker Soundcore Space Q45 is the budget king. The ANC is genuinely impressive for the price. On a 3-hour domestic flight, it killed about 85% of the engine noise. It's not Sony or Bose level, but it's way better than anything else under $150.

The ear cups are comfortable for about 4 hours, then they start to feel warm. The clamping pressure is moderate — fine for most heads, but not as gentle as the Bose. Battery life is a monster at 50 hours with ANC off and 40 hours with ANC on. That's better than both premium options.

Build quality feels solid but plastic. The hinge mechanism is a bit creaky. Call quality is acceptable for quick calls, but background noise handling is poor compared to the Sony.

Pros: Unbeatable price, 40-hour battery with ANC, decent voice cancellation for the price, USB-C fast charging

Cons: ANC struggles with sudden loud noises, ear cups get warm, no multipoint connection for 2 devices

Bose QuietComfort Ultra

Price: $349 - $429 | Rating: 4.5/5

For the last year, Bose has been selling the QuietComfort Ultra alongside the QC45. The Ultra adds spatial audio and improved ANC, but it costs $100 more. For airplane noise specifically, the difference is minimal. The Ultra cancels slightly more high-frequency noise, but the QC45 handles the engine drone just as well.

Where the Ultra shines is comfort. It uses a slimmer headband design that distributes weight better than the QC45. After 10 hours, I had zero pressure points. That's impressive. But if you're strictly looking for airplane ANC and don't care about spatial audio, save the $100 and get the QC45.

Pros: Best-in-class comfort, slightly better ANC than QC45, immersive audio mode for movies

Cons: Expensive, spatial audio is a gimmick for music (great for movies though), battery life still 24 hours

Frequently Asked Questions

Which has better noise cancellation on a plane: Sony or Bose?

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is better for airplane noise. It cancels more low-frequency engine rumble and significantly better mid-frequency sounds like voices and crying babies. The Bose is close, but the Sony's dual-processor system is a clear step ahead.

Can I sleep with these headphones on?

Yes, but the Bose QC45 is better for side sleepers because it's lighter and has a softer clamp. The Sony's firmer headband makes it less ideal for sleeping on your side. The Anker is too bulky for side sleeping.

Do I need to turn ANC off to save battery on long flights?

No. Both the Sony and Bose have enough battery to last a full 14-hour flight with ANC on. If you're doing back-to-back ultra-long-haul flights without charging, then consider the Anker's 40-hour battery instead.

Will these work with the airplane's inflight entertainment system?

Yes, but you'll need a Bluetooth transmitter. Most planes don't have Bluetooth audio support yet. The Sony and Bose both support wired mode, but you lose ANC when using the included cable. You can buy a Bluetooth transmitter that supports the aptX Low Latency codec to avoid audio delay.

Are these good for calls in the airport?

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the best for calls. Its beamforming microphones isolate your voice even in busy terminals. The Bose QC45 is acceptable but lets more background noise through. The Anker is only good for quiet rooms.

The Bottom Line

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5 if you want the absolute quietest flight possible. It's the best ANC you can buy, and the voice reduction is a game-changer for anyone who hates crying babies or loud talkers. Just be prepared for the higher price and the bulkier case that won't fold flat. Buy the Bose QC45 if you fly ultra-long-haul and comfort is your top priority. The 238-gram weight and gentle clamp make it the only headphone I can wear for 10+ hours without thinking about it. It's the better travel companion for most people, even if the Sony is technically better at noise cancellation. And if you're on a budget, the Anker Soundcore Space Q45 gets you 85% of the way there for a third of the price — just don't expect miracles on an 8-hour red-eye.
