Why Flat Faces Need Different Slow Feeders

Brachycephalic dogs have shortened muzzles. That means their tongue is longer relative to their face, and they can't easily lick the bottom of a deep bowl. Most slow feeders are designed for long-nosed dogs like labs and retrievers. They have tall ridges and deep wells. For a pug, those ridges block the mouth entirely. The dog gives up, flips the bowl, or eats around the edges — defeating the purpose.

The fix is simple: shallow, wide bowls with low, rounded bumps. The dog's tongue can sweep over the surface, but they can't just gulp. You want a bowl where the food sits in a single layer, not stacked.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Depth and Ridge Height

Measure the distance from your dog's nose tip to their eyes. That's the maximum ridge height they can handle. For most flat-faced dogs, ridges should be under 1 inch tall. Anything taller becomes a wall, not a speed bump. Look for bowls with gentle, rounded curves — not sharp peaks.

Material Matters

Stainless steel is easiest to clean but slippery. Silicone is grippy but can tear. BPA-free plastic is fine if it's food-grade and thick enough not to crack. Avoid ceramic with painted designs — the paint chips and your dog eats it. Look for FDA-approved materials and dishwasher-safe claims.

Wobble vs. Stationary

Wobble bowls force the dog to stabilize the bowl with their paws while eating. This adds a mental challenge that naturally slows them down. But some flat-faced dogs get frustrated and knock the bowl over. If your dog is easily frustrated, start with a stationary bowl with a heavy base and anti-skid ring.

Cleaning

Slow feeders have nooks and crannies. If it's not dishwasher-safe, you'll be hand-washing grease and kibble dust every day. Check that the bowl is truly top-rack dishwasher safe, not just "wipe clean." We tested all bowls with peanut butter and wet food to see how they cleaned up.

Size and Capacity

Most flat-faced dogs are small to medium. You need a bowl that holds 1 to 2 cups of kibble but keeps it in a single layer. If a bowl is too big, the dog can just push food to one side and gulp. If too small, food spills over the edges. Look at the diameter — 6 to 8 inches is the sweet spot for pugs and Frenchies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a slow feeder fix my dog's vomiting?

Most of the time, yes. If your dog vomits right after eating because they're gulping air, a slow feeder will reduce that. But if vomiting continues after two weeks of using a slow feeder, see your vet. It could be acid reflux or a food allergy, not just speed.

How long should a slow feeder meal take?

Ideally, 2 to 4 minutes. If your dog finishes in under a minute, the bowl isn't challenging enough. If they take longer than 10 minutes, they're probably frustrated, not eating. Adjust by spreading food thinner or using wet food.

Can I use a slow feeder for puppies?

Yes, but start with the gentlest option — the Dogit Go Slow or the Lickimat. Puppies have smaller tongues and weaker jaw muscles. A complex maze might cause them to skip meals. Supervise the first few uses.

How do I clean a slow feeder properly?

Rinse immediately after use. Then either run through the dishwasher (top rack) or hand-wash with hot soapy water. Use a bottle brush to get into the ridges. Dry completely before storing to prevent mold in the crevices.

What if my dog flips the bowl?

Get a bowl with a heavier base or suction ring. The Leash & Co. and Neater Pets are the heaviest we tested. You can also place the bowl in a larger, empty baking dish to add stability.
