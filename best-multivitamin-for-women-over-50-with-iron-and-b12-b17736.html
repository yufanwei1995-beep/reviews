Most "50+" multivitamins cut iron out completely. The logic? Post-menopausal women don't lose blood monthly, so they need less iron. But that logic fails if you're still having periods, have been diagnosed with iron-deficiency anemia, or simply don't absorb iron well from food alone.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before we get to the reviews, you need to know what separates a good women's 50+ multivitamin from a waste of money.

Iron: Form and Dose Matter

Look for ferrous bisglycinate (chelated iron). It's gentler on the stomach and absorbs much better than ferrous sulfate, which causes constipation in most women over 50. The dose should be between 8 mg and 18 mg. More than that can cause nausea, and less than 8 mg won't help if you're actually deficient.

B12: Methylated is Best

As you age, your stomach acid drops. That makes it harder to absorb B12 from food or from cheap forms like cyanocobalamin. Look for methylcobalamin or adenosylcobalamin — these are the active forms your body can use immediately without conversion. Avoid products that list only cyanocobalamin.

Vitamin A: Avoid Excess Retinol

Too much preformed vitamin A (retinol) can weaken bones over time. Post-menopausal women already have higher osteoporosis risk. Look for beta-carotene (provitamin A) instead of high-dose retinol.

Folate vs. Folic Acid

Choose methylfolate (5-MTHF) over synthetic folic acid. Up to 40% of women have a MTHFR gene variation that prevents them from converting folic acid into usable folate. The active form bypasses that issue entirely.

Third-Party Testing

Look for USP, NSF, or Informed Choice certification. These ensure the label matches what's actually in the bottle. It's an extra layer of protection that matters more with supplements than almost any other product category.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe to take iron after menopause?

It depends. If you're post-menopausal and not anemic, extra iron can build up in your tissues and cause oxidative stress. But if you have a confirmed deficiency, it's safe and necessary. Get a ferritin blood test before you start any iron-containing multi.

Can I take this with my thyroid medication?

No. Iron blocks thyroid hormone absorption. Take your thyroid med in the morning, wait 4 hours, then take your multivitamin. Same rule applies to calcium supplements.

How much B12 do I actually need after 50?

The RDA is 2.4 mcg, but that's the minimum to prevent deficiency. Most experts recommend 25-50 mcg daily for women over 50 because absorption declines with age. The active methyl form is absorbed up to 3x better than cyanocobalamin.

Why does my current multi cause constipation?

It's almost certainly the iron form. Ferrous sulfate is cheap but harsh on the gut. Switch to ferrous bisglycinate or a whole-food source. That usually fixes the problem within a week.
