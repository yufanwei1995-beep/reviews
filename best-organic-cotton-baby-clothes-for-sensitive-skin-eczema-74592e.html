If your baby has eczema, you already know the drill: red, itchy patches, sleepless nights, and a laundry basket full of half-tried "gentle" fabrics. Conventional cotton is often treated with formaldehyde resins, synthetic dyes, and chemical softeners that can strip moisture and trigger flare-ups. Organic cotton skips the pesticides and harsh finishes, but not all organic brands are created equal.

I tested five popular organic cotton clothing lines on my own kids (and a few willing friends) over the past two months. I washed them repeatedly, checked for pilling, and most importantly, watched for skin reactions. Here’s what actually held up.

What to Look For in Eczema-Safe Clothing

Not every label that says "organic" is safe for sensitive skin. Here’s the checklist I used.

1. The Certification Matters

GOTS (Global Organic Textile Standard) is the gold standard. It bans toxic chemicals at every stage, from farming to finishing. "100% organic cotton" without a certification can still be processed with harsh detergents. Look for the GOTS logo on the tag or product page.

2. Weave and Weight

Eczema skin needs smooth, tight weaves. Loose knits rub and irritate. Interlock knit (double-layered) is the best for pajamas because it’s smooth on both sides. Single jersey (like basic t-shirts) is fine for daytime but can be scratchy after washing.

3. Dyes and Prints

Even "low-impact" dyes can be problematic. The safest bets are undyed (natural cream or brown cotton) or garments dyed with plant-based pigments. Avoid screen-printed graphics—they sit on top of the fabric and crack, creating rough patches.

4. Seams and Tags

Flat seams are non-negotiable. Regular overlock seams leave a raised ridge that rubs raw on eczema patches. Tags should be printed on the fabric, not sewn in. Check the inside of the neck and cuffs closely.

5. Fit

Too tight traps heat and sweat, which worsens itch. Too loose means fabric bunches and rubs. Look for snug but not compressive fits, especially for sleepwear.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is organic cotton enough for eczema?

It's the foundation, but not the whole solution. You also need to avoid tight elastics, rough seams, and thick fabrics that trap sweat. I've seen babies flare up in certified organic clothes that were just too warm.

How many layers should an eczema baby wear?

One more layer than you feel comfortable in. Overheating is the #1 trigger for nighttime itch. If the room is 70°F, a single organic cotton footie is plenty.

Can I use regular laundry detergent on organic clothes?

You can, but you shouldn't. Standard detergents leave a residue that builds up and stiffens the fabric. Use a free-and-clear detergent without enzymes or optical brighteners. Wash on cold, and skip the fabric softener—it coats the fibers and blocks breathability.

Do I need to pre-wash organic cotton?

Yes. Organic cotton can still have natural plant waxes and dust from processing. Wash once before first wear with a fragrance-free detergent. It won't shrink much if it's pre-shrunk, but the texture improves dramatically.

What about wool or bamboo for eczema?

Wool is a natural antimicrobial, but many eczema kids react to lanolin. Bamboo (viscose) is soft but processed with chemicals that don't meet organic standards. Stick with certified organic cotton for the safest bet.
