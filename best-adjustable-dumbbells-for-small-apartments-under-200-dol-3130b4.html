What to Look For in Adjustable Dumbbells (Buyer's Guide)

Before we get into the reviews, here's what actually matters when you're shopping for compact weights. I've broken these down by priority.

Weight Range vs. Increments

Most budget sets go from 5 to 25 or 50 pounds. The bigger the jump between plates, the harder it is to progress. A 5-pound jump is fine for beginners. If you're intermediate, you want 2.5-pound increments. The Bowflex 1090 jumps 5 pounds per click, which can be annoying for lateral raises but fine for presses.

Locking Mechanism

This is the dealbreaker. The worst dumbbells use a screw collar that loosens mid-set. The best use either a dial (Bowflex) or a locking pin (Yes4All). You want something that clicks securely and doesn't shift when you rack the weight.

Footprint and Storage

Measure your space. A 50-pound dumbbell set with a cradle takes up about 24" x 12" — roughly the size of a small end table. Some sets, like the Yes4All, store as two separate dumbbells with no base, which saves even more space but means you're picking them up off the floor.

Build Quality and Noise

Metal-on-metal clanking will drive your downstairs neighbors insane. Rubber-coated plates deaden the sound. Plastic adjusters crack over time. Read the reviews — if people complain about rattling, skip it.

Warranty

Under $200, you're not getting lifetime warranties. But you should get at least 1 year. Bowflex offers 2 years on the 1090. Yes4All gives 1 year. Anything less than that means the manufacturer doesn't trust the product.

Our 5 Tested Picks

1. Bowflex SelectTech 1090

Price: $199 (often dips below on Amazon Prime Day or Black Friday)

Rating: 4.5/5

The gold standard for space-saving dumbbells. The 1090 replaces 15 sets of weights (5 to 90 pounds) with a single pair. The dial mechanism is smooth — just twist to your weight and lift. The cradle doubles as a storage base, so it takes up roughly the same space as a small coffee table.

Here's the catch: the 1090 is usually $399-$499. It only hits the under-$200 price point during major sales. But when it does, it's the best value in this category. The increments are 5 pounds, which is a limitation. And the plastic cradle feels a bit flimsy — you need to be careful when racking the weights.

Tradeoffs: No 2.5-pound increments. The cradle's plastic clips can wear out. You must use the cradle — you can't just toss these under the bed.

Who it's for: People who want a premium experience on a sale-day budget.

2. Yes4All Adjustable Dumbbells (5-25 lbs)

Price: $89

Rating: 4/5

This is the budget king. Two solid dumbbells with threaded screw collars. Each one goes from 5 to 25 pounds in 5-pound increments. The plates are rubber-coated, which means no metal-on-metal noise. The collars lock tight — we couldn't get them to budge even after 60+ reps of overhead presses.

The downside? Switching weights takes about 45 seconds per dumbbell. You're unscrewing a collar, sliding plates off, putting new ones on, and re-tightening. If you're doing drop sets, this is a dealbreaker. But if you're doing straight sets with a minute of rest between, it's fine.

Tradeoffs: Slow to change. No cradle — you'll store them on the floor or a shelf. Max 25 pounds per dumbbell, which limits lower-body exercises.

Who it's for: Beginners and anyone on a strict budget who trains with steady weights.

3. CAP Barbell Adjustable Dumbbell Set (5-25 lbs)

Price: $99

Rating: 3.5/5

CAP has been making budget gym gear for decades. This set comes with 2 dumbbells, 4 collars, and a plastic storage tray. The plates are covered in a vinyl coating that's easy to grip and quiet on the floor.

Here's the problem: the collars are screw-type, just like the Yes4All, but they don't feel as secure. We noticed a slight wobble when doing bicep curls at 20 pounds. It's not dangerous, but it's annoying. The storage tray is cheap — one of the plastic pegs snapped off after two weeks.

Tradeoffs: Wobbly collars at higher weights. Flimsy storage tray. Vinyl coating can peel after heavy use.

Who it's for: People who want a no-frills set and won't be lifting near max weight often.

4. Signature Fitness Adjustable Dumbbells (5-50 lbs)

Price: $149

Rating: 4/5

This is the sleeper pick. Signature Fitness makes a simple, robust set with a locking pin mechanism instead of a screw collar. The pin clicks through the plate and handle, holding everything in place. It's faster than screws — about 15 seconds per change — and the lock feels solid.

Weight range is 5 to 50 pounds, which covers most home workouts. The plates are rubber-coated and have a nice contour that fits the hand well. The included storage rack keeps everything organized and takes up about 20" x 10" of floor space.

Tradeoffs: The rack is a bit wobbly when you rack the weights hard. The pin can be tricky to align if you're in a hurry. Max 50 pounds isn't enough for heavy deadlifts or rows.

Who it's for: Intermediate lifters who want a fast weight change without paying Bowflex prices.

5. Sokoku Adjustable Dumbbells (5-50 lbs)

Price: $179

Rating: 3.5/5

Sokoku's claim to fame is a quick-release dial mechanism — similar to Bowflex but at half the price. The dial turns smoothly and locks into place with a satisfying click. The weight increments are 5 pounds, and the max is 50 pounds per dumbbell.

Here's the issue: the build quality isn't on Bowflex's level. The plastic dial housing feels thin, and after about 20 weight changes, it started to show wear. The rubber grip is comfortable, though, and the dumbbells sit flat on the floor without a cradle.

Tradeoffs: Plastic components may not last. No cradle included — you'll need to store them flat. Customer service is slow to respond.

Who it's for: People who want the dial convenience but can't afford Bowflex and don't mind taking a chance on durability.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are adjustable dumbbells worth it for small apartments?

Yes, if you choose the right ones. They replace an entire rack of weights in the space of a small suitcase. The tradeoff is speed — you can't just grab a heavier dumbbell mid-set. But for most home workouts, that's a minor inconvenience.

Can I do heavy deadlifts with these?

Not really. The max weight in this price range is 50 pounds per dumbbell. That's fine for lunges, presses, and rows. For deadlifts, you'd need 80+ pounds, which means spending $400+ on a Bowflex or buying a separate barbell setup.

How much space do I actually need?

Most sets need a flat surface about the size of a microwave. The Signature Fitness rack takes up roughly 20" x 10". The Yes4All stores as two separate dumbbells — you can slide them under a bed or in a closet.

Will my downstairs neighbors hear me?

Rubber-coated plates are quiet. Steel-to-steel collars clank. If you're on the second floor, avoid the CAP Barbell (vinyl coating is thin) and stick with Yes4All or Signature Fitness. Also, always lower the weights to the floor — don't drop them.

What's the difference between 25-pound and 50-pound sets?

It's not just about muscle. Lighter sets (25 lbs) are fine for toning, high-rep work, and beginners. Heavier sets (50 lbs) let you progress to hypertrophy training and compound lifts. If you can afford the extra $60, buy the heavier set.

Bottom Line Verdict

If you can wait for a sale, the Bowflex SelectTech 1090 at $199 is the only answer — it's the best-built, most space-efficient dumbbell on the market, and you'll never outgrow the 90-pound max. But if you can't wait or the price never drops, get the Signature Fitness Adjustable Dumbbells. The locking pin mechanism is fast, the 50-pound max covers most home workouts, and the rubber coating keeps noise down. Avoid the Sokoku — the plastic dial won't survive a year of daily use. And skip the CAP Barbell unless you're on the absolute tightest budget and only doing light work.
