If you're serious about pulling great shots at home without dropping $3,000 on a commercial rig, you've probably narrowed it down to two names: Breville and DeLonghi. These two brands dominate the home espresso market, but they take very different approaches. Breville focuses on precise temperature control and built-in grinders. DeLonghi leans on traditional Italian brewing and user-friendly automation.

I've spent the last month pulling shots and steaming milk on five of the most popular models from both brands. This guide breaks down which one actually fits your kitchen, your skill level, and your morning routine. No fluff, just the real differences.

Breville vs DeLonghi: What to Look For

Before you spend money, understand what actually matters in a home espresso machine. These five factors separate a toy from a tool.

1. Grinder Quality

Espresso is 90% grind consistency. A machine with a weak grinder will produce bitter or sour shots no matter how good its boiler is. Breville's built-in conical burr grinders are genuinely decent — they offer 30 to 60 grind settings and produce consistent particle size. DeLonghi's built-in grinders, especially on cheaper models like the Stilosa, are often stepped burr grinders that produce more fines and clumping. If you buy a DeLonghi, plan on buying a separate grinder.

2. Temperature Stability

This is where Breville truly shines. Their PID (Proportional-Integral-Derivative) controller keeps water temperature within ±1°F. That's a huge deal because espresso extracts best at exactly 195°F to 205°F. DeLonghi uses a simpler thermostat that swings more — you'll taste the difference as sour or burnt notes, especially on back-to-back shots.

3. Milk Steaming Power

If you drink lattes or cappuccinos, this matters more than anything. Breville's steam wands use a 1600W thermocoil heating system that produces dry, powerful steam. DeLonghi's Dedica and Stilosa models have weaker steam pressure — you'll get microfoam, but it takes practice and patience. The DeLonghi La Specialista Arte has a better steam wand, but it's still not as powerful as Breville's.

4. Ease of Use vs. Control

DeLonghi focuses on one-touch automation. The Dedica has preset buttons for espresso and cappuccino. The La Specialista has a "Smart Tamp" that measures pressure for you. That's great for beginners. Breville gives you full manual control — you choose grind size, dose, and extraction time. That's great for learners who want to improve, but it has a steeper learning curve.

5. Build Quality and Footprint

Breville machines are heavier and feel more solid. The Barista Express weighs 23 pounds and has a stainless steel body. DeLonghi's Dedica is lighter (9.5 pounds) and has more plastic. If counter space is tight, the Dedica's 6-inch width is a huge win. If you want something that will last a decade, Breville wins.

"The single biggest mistake people make is buying a cheap machine with a bad grinder. You'll fight it every single day." — A barista friend who's been in the industry for 15 years.

Individual Product Reviews

1. Breville Barista Express (BES870XL)

Best for: Serious home baristas who want a complete package.

The Barista Express is the machine I recommend to 8 out of 10 people. It has a 1600W thermocoil heating system, a PID temperature controller, and a conical burr grinder built right in. You can go from whole beans to a perfect shot in under 60 seconds.

The grinder has 30 settings, which is enough to dial in most beans. The steam wand is powerful and produces silky microfoam — I made latte art on my first try after a week of practice. The 67oz water tank is removable and easy to fill.

But it's not perfect. The grinder can clog if you use very oily beans. The group head is a 54mm size, not the standard 58mm, which limits your aftermarket portafilter options. And it takes up a lot of counter space (13.5 inches wide, 13 inches deep).

Bottom line: If you want one machine that does everything well, this is it. It's the best value in home espresso.

2. Breville Barista Pro (BES878)

Best for: People who want faster heat-up time and a better grinder.

The Barista Pro is the Express's big brother. It heats up in 3 seconds (versus the Express's 30-45 seconds) thanks to a ThermoJet heating system. The grinder is also upgraded — it has a 10-position dial with micro-adjustments, giving you 60 total settings. That's a noticeable improvement in dialing in different beans.

The LCD screen shows you a shot timer and pressure gauge, which is helpful for learning. The steam wand is identical to the Express, which means it's excellent.

The downside? It costs about $300 more than the Express. And the 54mm portafilter is still a limitation. But if you make multiple drinks daily, the faster heat-up and finer grind control are worth it.

Bottom line: A better machine than the Express, but only worth the upgrade if you're making 3+ drinks a day.

3. DeLonghi La Specialista Arte (EC9155MB)

Best for: Beginners who want automated assistance.

The La Specialista Arte is DeLonghi's answer to the Barista Express. It has a built-in conical burr grinder with 8 settings, and its "Smart Tamp" levels and tamps the coffee for you. That's a game-changer for people who struggle with consistent tamping pressure.

The machine uses a 15-bar pump and has a "Active Temperature Control" system that's better than DeLonghi's cheaper models, but it's still not as precise as Breville's PID. I found the shots to be good but slightly less consistent than the Barista Express.

The steam wand is improved over the Dedica — it produces decent microfoam, but it takes longer to texture milk compared to Breville. The machine also has a bypass doser for pre-ground coffee, which is handy for decaf or a different roast.

One big win: it's $150 cheaper than the Barista Express. And it looks great on the counter with its stainless steel and matte black finish.

Bottom line: The easiest machine to learn on, but you'll hit its limits faster if you get serious about espresso.

4. DeLonghi Dedica (EC680M)

Best for: Small kitchens and budget buyers.

The Dedica is a slim, 6-inch wide machine that's perfect for tight spaces. It costs around $300, making it the most affordable option here. It has a 15-bar pump and a thermoblock heating system that heats up in about 30 seconds.

But there are real compromises. The included pressurized baskets make it forgiving for beginners, but they produce a fake crema that lacks the depth of real espresso. The steam wand is weak — you can make milk foam, but it takes practice to get anything close to microfoam. And there's no built-in grinder, so you'll need to buy one separately.

Honestly, most people who buy the Dedica end up upgrading within a year. It's fine for a first machine, but it's not a long-term solution.

Bottom line: Buy this only if you're on a tight budget and okay with learning on a limited machine.

5. Breville Bambino Plus (BES500BSS)

Best for: People who already own a grinder.

The Bambino Plus is the best machine Breville makes for under $500. It uses the same ThermoJet heating system as the Barista Pro, so it's ready in 3 seconds. It has an automatic milk foaming feature that produces excellent microfoam at the touch of a button — perfect for beginners.

The catch: there's no grinder. You need to own a separate grinder, which adds at least $200 to your setup. If you already have one, this is the best value in the lineup. If you don't, the Barista Express is a better deal.

The Bambino Plus is also compact (7.7 inches wide) and comes in a range of colors. The water tank is smaller (47oz), but that's fine for most homes.

Bottom line: The smartest purchase for people who want a dedicated grinder and a fast, reliable machine.

FAQ

Which brand makes better espresso?

Breville. Their PID temperature control and better grinders produce more consistent, flavorful shots. DeLonghi's machines are more forgiving but less precise.

Is DeLonghi good for beginners?

Yes, especially the La Specialista Arte. The automatic tamping and preset buttons reduce the learning curve. But you'll likely outgrow it faster than a Breville.

Do I need a separate grinder?

Only if you buy the Dedica or Bambino Plus. The Barista Express, Barista Pro, and La Specialista Arte all have built-in grinders. But a separate grinder (like the Baratza Encore ESP) is always better for serious espresso.

Which machine is easier to clean?

DeLonghi machines are easier to wipe down because they have fewer exposed parts. Breville machines have more removable parts but are still manageable. Plan on doing a deep clean every month either way.

Can I make a latte with the Dedica?

Yes, but the steam wand is weak. You'll get frothy milk, not silky microfoam. It works, but it's not great.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Breville Barista Express (BES870XL). It's the best balance of price, performance, and convenience for a home barista. The built-in grinder is good enough to produce excellent shots, the PID controller keeps temperature stable, and the steam wand can handle any milk drink you throw at it. It's not the cheapest option, but it's the one that will make you happy every morning for years.

If you're on a tight budget or have zero counter space, the DeLonghi Dedica is a fine starting point — just know you'll want to upgrade. If you already own a good grinder, skip everything else and get the Breville Bambino Plus for its instant heat-up and automatic milk frothing. But for most people, the Barista Express is the answer.
