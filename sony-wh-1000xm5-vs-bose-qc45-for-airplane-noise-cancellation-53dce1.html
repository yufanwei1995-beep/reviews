Who This Is For and What Problem This Solves

You fly. A lot. You are tired of cranking the volume to 80% just to drown out the jet engine hum, the screaming toddler three rows back, and the guy who thinks the armrest is his personal property. This is a head-to-head comparison of the two biggest names in noise-cancelling headphones: the Sony WH-1000XM5 and the Bose QuietComfort 45. I took both on a brutal 6-hour transcontinental flight, a cramped regional jet, and spent an afternoon in a busy airport terminal to see which one actually saves your sanity.

Quick Summary
  • Who this is for: Frequent flyers and commuters who prioritize silence over everything else.
  • Our top pick: Sony WH-1000XM5 — it cancels more low-frequency engine roar and adapts better to changing cabin noise.
  • Price range: $298-$428 depending on sales and colorway for both models.

Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters for Airplane Noise

Before you drop $300+, you need to understand what makes a good airplane headphone. It's not just about the marketing buzzwords.

Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) Depth

The best ANC for planes targets low-frequency sounds (the "rumble" of engines). The Sony WH-1000XM5 uses two processors and eight microphones to handle this. The Bose QC45 uses a simpler, but proven, system. In my tests, Sony wins on pure low-frequency attenuation — the cabin rumble drops to a whisper. Bose is better at handling sudden, mid-range noises like a dropped tray table or a loud conversation, but it lets more of that constant drone through.

Comfort Over Long Hauls

You will wear these for 5+ hours. The Bose QC45 is lighter at 240g vs the Sony's 250g. That 10g difference matters on the crown of your head. The Bose has a more classic, clamp-free fit. The Sony has wider, deeper earcups that fit larger ears better, but the headband pressure is slightly more noticeable after hour four. If you have a smaller head, Bose wins. If you have big ears, Sony wins.

Battery Life and Charging Speed

Both claim 30+ hours. Real world? The Sony WH-1000XM5 gets about 28 hours with ANC on at moderate volume. The Bose QC45 hits about 26 hours. The difference is the charging port. Sony uses USB-C (standard for 2026). Bose also uses USB-C, but the QC45 charges slower — a 15-minute quick charge gets you 3 hours on Sony, but only 2.5 on Bose.

Call Quality in Noisy Environments

This is where most people get burned. You think you're buying headphones for music, but you end up taking calls in the airport. The Sony WH-1000XM5 has a beamforming mic setup that isolates your voice from background noise. The Bose QC45 sounds muffled to the person on the other end if you're near an air conditioning vent or a crowd. I tested both at Gate B12 — my wife said I sounded "clear and close" on the Sony, and "like I was in a fish tank" on the Bose.

In-Depth Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $348-$428 | Rating: 4.8/5

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the king of silence. The ANC is not just "good" — it's borderline magical. The V1 processor and QN1 chip work together to cancel out the specific frequency of the plane's engines. On my 6-hour flight, I forgot the engines were on. That's the highest compliment I can give.

The sound profile is rich and detailed. Bass is punchy but not muddy. Vocals are crisp. The 30mm drivers are a step up from the XM4's 40mm, but they sound more refined, not louder. You will hear string instruments and background details you missed before.

Comfort is good, but not perfect. The earcups are plush and deep. My ears didn't touch the inside. The headband is the weak spot — it has a bit more pressure than the Bose. After 4 hours, I felt it. Not painful, just present.

The bad news: The hinge design is fragile. There are multiple reports of the swivel joint snapping. I haven't broken mine, but I treat them like glass. Also, there is no IP rating for sweat or water. Don't wear these to the gym.

Call quality: Excellent. The four mics pick up your voice and suppress background noise. A call in a busy terminal sounded clear to the other person.

Verdict: If you want the absolute best noise cancellation money can buy and you're gentle with your gear, this is it.

Bose QuietComfort 45

Price: $298-$329 | Rating: 4.5/5

The Bose QC45 is the comfort king. At 240g, it's the lightest flagship ANC headphone you can buy. The clamping force is minimal. The ear cushions are soft and breathable. I fell asleep wearing these on a 3-hour regional flight — that never happens.

ANC performance is solid, but a step behind Sony. The QC45 cancels the mid-range and high-frequency noises better than the XM5. You will hear less of the "shhh" of the air vents and the rustling of passengers. But the low-frequency engine rumble is more present. It's not annoying, but you know it's there.

Sound quality is neutral and flat. That's Bose's style. There's no artificial bass boost. If you like a "pure" sound, you'll love it. If you like hip-hop or EDM, you'll be bored. The bass is there, but it's polite, not powerful.

The bad news: The microphone quality is a letdown. On calls, my voice sounded distant and echoey. The Bose Music app is also basic — no custom EQ for the QC45. You get three presets: Bass Boost, Treble Boost, and Flat. That's it.

Verdict: If comfort is your #1 priority and you fly for 8+ hours at a stretch, the QC45 is your best friend.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra (The Alternative)

Price: $429-$449 | Rating: 4.7/5

If you're torn between the two, the Bose QC Ultra is the compromise. It takes the QC45's comfortable frame and adds Sony-level ANC. Bose's "CustomTune" technology actually measures your ear canal and adjusts the noise cancellation in real-time. It's scary good.

It also adds spatial audio, which is a gimmick for music but great for movies. The battery life is 24 hours (less than both others), and the price is higher. But if you want Bose comfort and Sony silence, this is the one.

Verdict: Only buy this if you need the absolute best of both worlds and don't care about the price tag.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better for sleeping on a plane?

The Bose QC45. It's lighter and has less clamping force. You can wear it for 10 hours without noticing it. The Sony is fine, but the headband pressure will wake you up on longer flights.

Can I use these with the in-flight entertainment system?

Yes, but you need a Bluetooth transmitter dongle. Neither headphone has a standard 3.5mm jack in the box. The Sony comes with a 3.5mm cable, but it's a weird proprietary plug. The Bose QC45 has a standard 3.5mm jack. If you fly on older planes, check the seat's jack type — the Bose is more universal.

Do these handle pressure changes in the cabin?

Both have automatic pressure relief. The Sony has a dedicated barometric pressure sensor that adjusts the ANC as the plane climbs and descends. The Bose does this too, but less aggressively. Neither caused "ear pop" discomfort during my flights.

Which one has the better app?

Sony's Headphones Connect app is far superior. You get a 5-band EQ, noise cancelling optimization, and wind reduction. Bose's app is minimal — you get a battery meter and a few presets. If you like tweaking sound, Sony wins.

Is the Sony WH-1000XM5 worth the extra $50 over the Bose QC45?

For airplane noise cancellation: yes. The low-frequency cancellation is noticeably better. For everything else: not necessarily. If you take more calls and value comfort, the Bose is the smarter buy.

The Bottom Line

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5. It cancels more airplane noise, sounds better, has a better app, and handles calls like a pro. The only reason to pick the Bose QC45 is if you have a smaller head, prioritize weight, or refuse to spend over $300. But for the specific job of killing jet engine rumble, the Sony is the undisputed winner in 2026. Just be careful with the hinges — put them in a hard case when you're not wearing them.
